System

Hardware

pogopo is built around a custom ESP32-S3 PCB with a 400 × 240 monochrome display, physical controls, audio, storage, motion sensing, haptics, and battery management.

System overview

The dual-core ESP32-S3 is paired with 16 MB of flash and 8 MB of Octal PSRAM. A Sharp LS027B7DH01 Memory LCD provides the 400 × 240 monochrome display, while a four-bit SDMMC bus connects removable storage without sharing the display’s SPI path.

Front controls arrive through a TCA9555 I/O expander. Motion data comes from a BMI270, audio leaves the MCU over I²S to a MAX98357A amplifier, and a BQ24295 manages charging and the power path. These choices keep the board compact while giving firmware direct control over timing-critical work.

Core components

ESP32-S3

The MCU runs PogopoOS, renders the interface, coordinates every peripheral, and hosts the Game Boy and PogoDate runtimes. GPIO, SPI, I²C, I²S, SDMMC, and ADC are all available directly from the chip, while FreeRTOS distributes time-sensitive work across both cores. It was chosen because it combines those interfaces with external-memory support and a mature ESP-IDF stack in one compact part.

Sharp LS027B7DH01 Memory LCD

The 400 × 240 panel displays the launcher, applications, games, diagnostics, and shutdown animation through SPI2 at 14 MHz. PogopoOS keeps a 12 KB 1-bit framebuffer, transfers only dirty rows when practical, and maintains VCOM in a dedicated task. The panel was chosen for its sharp monochrome image and its ability to retain static pixels without continuous full-screen refresh.

BMI270

The six-axis IMU reports acceleration and angular rate for diagnostics and motion-aware applications. It shares the I²C bus at address 0x68 and has a dedicated interrupt on GPIO18. This provides a compact motion input without consuming the GPIOs reserved for controls, display, storage, and audio.

MAX98357A audio

The MAX98357A converts the firmware’s PCM stream into amplified speaker output. DATA, BCLK, and LRCK connect directly to the ESP32-S3 I²S peripheral, and the Game Boy path runs at a fixed 32,768 Hz. A digital amplifier keeps sample timing under firmware control and avoids a separate analog DAC stage.

microSD storage

The microSD card holds ROMs, audio, Playdate packages, and other user content through ESP-IDF’s FAT filesystem. It uses a four-bit SDMMC connection on D0–D3, CLK, and CMD rather than a basic SPI link. That wider bus improves bulk transfer speed and leaves the display bus independent.

TCA9555 and controls

The TCA9555 reads the D-pad, A, B, Start, and Menu as one active-low byte over I²C at 0x20, with its interrupt connected to GPIO21. A failed read is treated as a released sample so a bus fault cannot leave a control stuck. The expander saves eight direct MCU pins and lets firmware capture the entire front-panel state in one transaction.

Haptics

The vibration motor is driven from GPIO3 through the board’s output stage. It gives UI and gameplay actions a short physical response, which is useful when another sound or screen effect would be distracting.

Battery and power management

The BQ24295 at I²C address 0x6B manages charging and the power path. Firmware reads its charge and fault state, measures the battery through a gated ADC on GPIO1, handles the separate Power input on GPIO17, and requests ship mode only after saves are flushed and the LCD is cleared. This gives PogopoOS a controlled shutdown path instead of relying on an abrupt battery disconnect.

Custom PCB

The custom board integrates the ESP32-S3, display connection, SDMMC bus, I²C peripherals, digital audio, controls, haptics, and power-management signals used by PogopoOS. Bring-up diagnostics probe the TCA9555, BMI270, and BQ24295 and print their register state to the serial monitor.

Enclosure

The enclosure is designed as a compact handheld around the display, physical controls, custom PCB, and a right-side expansion area. Mechanical module retention and the final external connector arrangement remain work in progress.
